# 14 · Questions to Ask the Hiring Manager

[← Index](00-Index.md) · **Manager-round priority:** High · **Read time:** ~5 min

> **Why this matters:** Your questions show how you think as a lead. Pick **4–5**, at least one each from *team*, *tech*, and *success*. Listen to the answers: they tell you which topics to emphasize in later rounds. Write down what you hear.

## Team (shows you're thinking like a lead)
1. "How big is the AI engineering team today, and what's the mix of seniority? How many people would I be leading or mentoring day to day?"
2. "What does the team need most from this role right now: technical direction, delivery, raising the bar on engineering practices, or growing junior engineers?"
3. "How does the AI engineering team work with data science, data engineering, and platform? Who owns what, for example evaluation or model deployment?"
4. "What does a great Lead AI Engineer here do that a good one doesn't?"

## Tech & platform (shows depth, and gets you intel for later rounds)
5. "The postings mention an agentic AI framework. Where is it today: early design, in production for some use cases, or being scaled to more teams?"
6. "Which cloud and model platforms are you standardizing on? The JD lists AWS, GCP, and Azure, so I'm curious what's actually in production."
7. "How do you evaluate GenAI systems today before and after release? Is there a shared eval harness or golden datasets?"
8. "How do compliance, privacy, and security review work for a new GenAI use case? How long does it typically take to get from prototype to production?"
9. "What's the hardest technical problem the team is facing right now?"

## Success & expectations
10. "What would make you say, six months from now, 'hiring this person was a great decision'?"
11. "How do you see the 40–50% hands-on split in practice? What would I typically be building myself?"
12. "Which use cases are highest priority this year: member-facing, clinical/utilization management, operations, or internal productivity?"

## Logistics (end of call, only if time allows; otherwise ask the recruiter)
13. "What are the next steps and timeline in the process?"
14. "The posting mentions in-office in Boston and also a Flex persona. What does the team's week actually look like?"

## Avoid
- Questions answered on the website or in the JD.
- Leading with salary or PTO in the manager round. Save those for the recruiter.
- "Do I have the job?" Instead, try: "Is there anything about my background you'd like me to clarify?" This surfaces concerns while you can still address them.

## Closing line (~20 sec)
> "Thanks, this was really helpful. What I'm hearing is that you need someone who can [repeat their top need], and that's exactly what I did with [one story]. I'm excited about this, and I'd love to continue the conversation."
